Why you get paid late (it's not just the client)

Surveys of freelancers keep finding the same thing: weeks every year lost to chasing invoices, and most freelancers feel uncomfortable doing it, so they put it off. Then they wait until they're angry, send a sharp email, and the relationship sours.

Most late payments aren't malicious. The invoice had no clear due date, went to the wrong person, or nobody reminded the client until it was already a problem. That's good news: it's a process problem, and process is fixable.

Rule 1 · Payment terms to agree before any work

  • Deposit up front: 30–50% before you start (100% for small jobs or new clients).
  • Due date in days: "payment due within 14 days of invoice". Shorter terms get paid faster.
  • Late fee: a fixed fee or a percentage per month. Check what's allowed where you and your client are.
  • Ownership on full payment: final files and usage rights transfer only when paid in full.
  • Right to pause: you can stop work if an invoice is more than X days overdue.

Copy-paste clause for your proposal, contract or first email:

A [50]% deposit is due before work begins. The balance is due within [14] days of the invoice date. Invoices unpaid after the due date incur a late fee of [2% per month / a fixed fee of X]. Ownership of final deliverables transfers to the client once payment is received in full. [Your name] may pause work on any project while an invoice is more than [14] days overdue.

This is a practical template, not legal advice. Late-fee rules differ by country and state, so check yours or ask a local professional for anything large.

Rule 2

The invoice checklist

Every missing item is an excuse to pay later. Run through this before you hit send.

  • A unique invoice number (INV-001, INV-002 …)
  • The due date written as a date ("Due 16 November 2026"), not just "Net 14"
  • Sent to the person who pays, with the person who hired you in CC
  • The client's PO or reference number, if they use one (big companies often won't pay without it)
  • What you did, in their words, with the date it was delivered
  • Every way to pay you: bank details, PayPal, Wise, payment link
  • Your late-fee line, matching the terms you agreed
  • Sent the same day you deliver. Every day you wait is a day added to when you get paid.
  • Logged in Paid On Time or the tracker the moment it's sent

Tough situations

When clients make excuses

"We're waiting to get paid by our client.""I understand. Our agreement is between us, though, and the invoice was due on [date]. Can you confirm a date you'll pay this week, or would a split payment help?"
"It's with accounts.""Thanks! Could you share the name or email of the person in accounts? I'll send it straight to them so it doesn't hold you up." Then send it to them directly.
"Cash is tight right now."Offer a payment plan in writing: "Happy to split it: [half] by [date] and the rest by [date]. Can you confirm?" A plan with dates beats an open promise.
Silence (no replies at all)Change channel. If you've emailed, send a WhatsApp or call. Then try someone else at the company. People ignore email more than they ignore a phone call.
"We're not happy with the work."Ask for specifics in writing straight away. Fix genuine issues fast. Point out that the work was approved on [date] if it was. Don't let a vague complaint quietly cancel the whole invoice.
They want more work while unpaid"Happy to start as soon as invoice [#] is settled." Never start new work for a client who already owes you.

After 30+ days

Stop all work, send the "Pause work" script, then decide whether the amount is worth formal recovery. Options include a formal letter of demand, a small-claims court, a collections agency, or the dispute process on the platform you were paid through. The rules differ by country, so look up your local process.
