## Business state

Continuing the "backtest audit wedge" brief. /money-strategy (fresh mode) tested the riskiest assumption, that traders will pay $49 to audit a TradingView strategy before buying it. Decision readiness failed on 3 of 4 signals (customer, channel, demand), so instead of a full strategy the session produced a 2-week two-track experiment. The user said yes to building Track B (seller outreach).

## Confirmed conclusions

- (new) Not decision-ready for a full strategy: can't name 3 buyers, no channel tried, no paid demand. Run experiments first.
- (new) The insight alone is free: TradingView's "Backtests Are Broken" community script already explains the trailing-stop / best-case-intrabar bias. What can be sold is applying it trade by trade to a specific script.
- (new) Second segment: strategy sellers may pay for an "independently audited" report (Myfxbook verified-badge precedent; TradingView publishing rules already demand realistic results).
- (new) Integrity rule for the seller track: publish every verdict, failures included, or the badge is worthless (rating-agency conflict).
- (new) Experiment: Track A buyers (free audits for 3, then $49 on day 14) and Track B sellers (20 messages offering a $99 audit).

## Ruled out

- Building the full 11-section strategy now: it would be guesswork until the experiment returns data.
- Relying on r/algotrading debunk posts alone: that audience already knows; script buyers mostly aren't there (atom A-b835).

## Open hypotheses

- Track A: pre-purchase buyers will pay. Pass if ≥1 pays $49, or ≥2 of 3 free-audit users say yes and name a script they were about to buy.
- Track B: sellers will pay for an audited badge. Pass if ≥2 of 20 reply with interest, or 1 pays $99.
- Refund guarantees (e.g. LuxAlgo 30-day) weaken the buyer-side pitch.
