The setups

  • V pattern and inverse. A swing low, a move at least one ATR away from it, then price comes back to retest the low without closing below it and prints a rejection candle: buy. Stop 5 points under the low, target the swing high in between. Only while price is above the day's open ("NASDAQ picks a direction for the day").
  • Z pattern. Price closes above the latest swing high, then within 30 bars pulls back to retest it and rejects: buy. Stop 5 points under the level, target the top made after the breakout. Same day-direction rule.
  • Support/resistance + rejection candle. A hammer whose low retests one of the last 10 swing lows: buy. Stop 5 points under the wick, target the nearest swing high above.

Sells mirror each rule. A swing is a high or low beyond the three bars on each side. A rejection candle closes back on the right side of the level, either in the trade's direction or with a wick at least twice its body.

What the tests show

  • Short charts lose most. On 15-minute bars the stops are tight, so a 2-point spread is a large share of each trade, and every setup loses 13–16% of its risk per trade.
  • The Z pattern wins often and still loses. It wins 54–63% of trades, but its targets are close and its losers are larger.
  • Rejection candles at support and resistance lose on every timeframe, even before costs.
  • No help from the NASDAQ's rise. The index roughly quadrupled over these years, and long trades still didn't produce a significant edge in any setup.

What isn't covered
  • The notes also use hand-drawn channels and triangles. Those rely on judgement and aren't coded here.
  • "Pick a direction for the day" is read as price being above (or below) the day's opening price at 00:00 UTC.
  • Prices are HistData NSXUSD 1-minute quotes (a NASDAQ-100 CFD), with no commission, overnight financing or news filter.
