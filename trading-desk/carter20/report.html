Why they fail

  • No edge to begin with. 19 of 20 lose or only break even before any costs. Only system 3 has a gain before costs that isn't chance (t = 4.2), and it's under half a pip.
  • Small targets, fixed costs. Many systems aim for 7–15 pips. A 1–1.5 pip spread takes 10–20% of every winner, and these systems trade hundreds to thousands of times a year.
  • High win rates don't help. Systems 3 and 19 win two trades in three, but their losers are twice the size of their winners.
  • Same in both halves. Train (2018 to mid-2022) and test (mid-2022 to 2026) results agree for every system. These are stable losses, not bad luck.

All 20 systems

"Per trade" is profit or loss as a % of the amount risked. "Account" is the change in balance over 8.7 years risking 1% per trade, compounded. Costs per round trip: EUR/USD 1.0 pip, USD/JPY 1.2, AUD/USD 1.3, GBP/USD 1.5, USD/CHF 1.6, GBP/JPY 2.8.

How the rules were read, and what isn't covered
  • Each system runs on the pairs the book lists. "Any pair" means EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY and AUD/USD.
  • Where the book gives a range, like a "10–15 pip stop", the midpoint is used. "Recent swing low/high" is the lowest low or highest high of the last 10 bars.
  • System 5 needs two custom indicators (GHL and QTI) that the book doesn't define, so it runs on its moving averages alone. System 15's arrow indicator is replaced by the close crossing the 15 EMA.
  • System 6 has no stop in the book. It uses a 100-pip emergency stop, so its "per trade" figure is measured against 100 pips.
  • Prices are HistData 1-minute bid quotes. Within a 5-minute bar, stops and targets fill in the order the minutes reached them. In the 1,179 cases where both fell inside one minute, the stop was taken.
  • No commissions, swaps, or news filters. Real trading would cost a little more, not less.
