Anti-Climax Backtest

The book's setup, coded exactly as written and tested on 10 crypto coins from 2020 to October 2026, on 15-minute and 1-hour candles. 179,516 trades on 15-minute candles, every one re-checked by an independent audit.

VERDICT · NO EDGE

Before costs the pattern breaks even at best. After a 0.05% fee and 2 bps slippage on each side it loses on every coin, in both time periods, on both timeframes, with or without the book's filters. Do not trade it as coded.

The pattern

  1. Pattern: each bar's high rises above the last by a bigger step than before (A < B < C). The top of the last bar is the limit line.
  2. Entry: sell stop at the low of the first bearish bar, either the pattern's last bar or the next one.
  3. Cancel: a bar trades entirely above the limit line, or 10 bars pass.
  4. Exit: stop at the limit line, target at 2× the risk, or close on an opposite pattern.

Longs mirror this with lows. The gated version adds the book's context rules: the pattern must not close beyond the last swing point, and the 50 EMA must slope with the trade.

Average result per trade

One R is the amount risked: entry to stop. Each run is shown before and after trading costs.

Best/worst: a candle doesn't show whether its high or low came first. When an entry and the stop both land in one bar, "worst" assumes the stop hit, "best" assumes it didn't. The truth sits between the two.

Why it fails

  • No edge before costs. Wins and losses net to about zero, so there's nothing for the fees to eat into.
  • Stops are tight. The median stop is 0.4–0.5% away on 15-minute bars, so the 0.14% round-trip cost alone is about 0.3R per trade.
  • The book's filters don't help. The gated version does no better than taking every pattern. A filter that adds nothing is the control result the method asks for.
  • Short holds. The median trade lasts 2–3 bars, and 60–70% of trades end at the stop.

All runs

What this test does and doesn't cover
  • Crypto, not the futures the book uses (CL, ES, NQ, 6A, FDAX). Crypto has no free intraday history for those markets here, and costs differ.
  • The book's author picks trades by hand using trend lines and support/resistance. EMA slope and swing pivots are stand-ins for that judgement, so a skilled discretionary trader could do better than this.
  • Today's top 10 coins were picked with hindsight, which flatters the result slightly. It still loses.
  • Entry and stop are at the exact level (zero-tick offset). The Pine script adds one tick each side.
